# Inducing Genuine Teamwork in a 3v3 rSoccer VSS PPO Self-Play Transformer

## TL;DR
- Naïve self-play with a single shared transformer will very likely reproduce the failure documented in rSoccer's own multi-agent VSSS benchmark — where "the results were worse than the respective single-agent ones… the agents did not learn to collaborate… the agents block each other" — so you must engineer teamwork with a layered stack: potential-based team-aware shaping, a staged curriculum plus a self-play league, attention/role auxiliary losses, and explicit multi-agent credit assignment.
- The single most reliable, theoretically safe lever is **potential-based reward shaping (PBRS, Ng et al. 1999)**: add shaping as F = γΦ(s′) − Φ(s), which provably leaves the optimal policy unchanged (and Devlin & Kudenko 2011 proved it preserves the Nash equilibria of a multi-agent Markov game); use it for spacing/positioning/possession-advancement potentials, and keep genuinely non-potential incentives (a discrete passing bonus) small and annealed.
- Because your transformer already emits a *centralized joint action* over a permutation-invariant token set, you are architecturally well-placed: adopt MAT-style advantage decomposition and a COMA-style counterfactual baseline for credit assignment, add a ROMA-style role-differentiation auxiliary loss to break weight-sharing symmetry, and copy published shaping constants from TiZero and Google Research Football rather than inventing your own.

## Key Findings

1. **The failure mode is documented in your exact environment.** The rSoccer paper (Martins et al. 2021, arXiv:2106.12895) reports verbatim that "In the multi-agent environments (IEEE VSSS and Pass Endurance)… the results were worse than the respective single-agent ones. This indicates that the agents did not learn to collaborate… in the VSSS, a visual inspection revels [sic] that, instead of collaborating, the agents block each other." That single-agent policy is the bar you must clear.

2. **Sparse reward alone fails; naïve dense cooperative shaping causes reward hacking or degenerate equilibria; PBRS plus competition/PBT is the escape.** DeepMind's soccer research shows both the danger (myopic, badly-scaled shaping "would induce degenerate behaviours") and the cure (competition + population-based training that automatically re-weights shaping toward the true objective). In simulated humanoid football, coordination is an *emergent phase transition*: "up to 1.5 billion environment steps… Division of labour decreases in this early phase as agents prioritize possession and learn uncoordinated ball chasing behaviours. After 1.5 billion environment steps a transition occurs in which division of labour improves and behaviour shifts from individual[istic ball chasing] to coordinated play."

3. **Concrete, copyable shaping constants exist.** Google Research Football's CHECKPOINT reward (+0.1 the first time the team possesses the ball in each of 10 goal-distance regions, capped at +1 = one goal) and TiZero's passing/spacing terms (holding-ball +0.0001/step, successful-pass-before-goal +0.05, grouping penalty −0.001 when teammates cluster) are directly portable to rSoccer VSS.

4. **Your centralized joint-action transformer is the right substrate for principled credit assignment.** MAT (Multi-Agent Transformer) casts joint policy as sequence modeling using the multi-agent advantage decomposition theorem; COMA gives a counterfactual baseline; QMIX/VDN give value decomposition; ROMA/RODE give emergent role specialization. All are compatible with a shared-weight token-set policy that outputs joint actions.

## Details

### Area 1 — Reward shaping for cooperation

**Potential-based reward shaping (PBRS) as the safe backbone.** Ng, Harada & Russell (1999, "Policy invariance under reward transformations: Theory and application to reward shaping") proved that adding a shaping term F(s,s′) = γΦ(s′) − Φ(s), for any bounded potential Φ: S → ℝ, leaves the set of optimal policies unchanged; the optimal value functions satisfy Q̃*(s,a) = Q*(s,a) − Φ(s). Devlin & Kudenko (2011, "Theoretical considerations of potential-based reward shaping for multi-agent systems," AAMAS) extended this to multi-agent settings, proving PBRS does not alter the Nash equilibria of the underlying Markov game (it can, beneficially, change *which* equilibrium is reached and speed exploration). Wiewiora (2003) proved PBRS is equivalent to initializing Q-values with Φ.

*Direct application to rSoccer VSS.* Define per-team potentials and add γΦ(s′) − Φ(s):
- **Ball-advancement potential:** Φ_ball = −‖ball − opp_goal‖ (the classic distance-to-goal heuristic Ng et al. endorse; essentially GRF CHECKPOINT made potential-based).
- **Spacing potential:** Φ_spacing = +(sum of pairwise teammate distances) or a target-formation potential; encourages spreading without a permanent additive bias, because as a potential difference it nets to zero over closed loops (no cycle-farming).
- **Positioning/possession potential:** Φ_pos rewarding a robot occupying open space or passing lanes; using a soccer-analytics value function Φ = EPV(state) (see Area 4) also qualifies as a state potential.

Because these are potential *differences*, they cannot be farmed by cycling — Ng et al.'s central guarantee — which is the theoretically clean way to inject positioning/spacing knowledge without overriding the ±1 scoring objective.

**Non-potential incentives (passing/assist bonuses) — keep small, keep annealed.** A discrete "+bonus for a completed pass" is not naturally potential-based and *can* change the optimal policy. The literature gives tested magnitudes:
- **TiZero (Lin et al. 2023, arXiv:2302.07515):** Holding-Ball +0.0001/timestep when your team controls the ball; **Passing-Ball +0.05 for a successful pass before a goal** (explicitly "encourages agents to learn coordinated passing strategies"); **Grouping penalty −0.001** when teammates gather too closely ("encourages agents to spread out"); sparse ±1 for scoring/conceding; all teammates receive team reward equally.
- **Google Research Football CHECKPOINT (Kurach et al. 2020, arXiv:1907.11180):** field split into 10 regions by Euclidean distance to opponent goal; **+0.1 the first time the team possesses the ball in each region, capped at +1**; uncollected checkpoints paid out on scoring so shooters aren't penalized; given once per episode.
- **DeepMind humanoid football (Liu et al. 2021, arXiv:2105.12196):** the base dense channels are deliberately myopic — from the companion paper "Emergent Coordination Through Competition" (arXiv:1902.07151), verbatim: "vel-to-ball: player's linear velocity projected onto its unit direction vector towards the ball, thresholded at zero; vel-ball-to-goal: ball's linear velocity projected onto its unit direction vector towards the center of opponent's goal" — plus the sparse ±1. Critically, an ablation that *added* two team-level coordination shaping rewards — verbatim "vel-ball-to-teammate-after-kick, encourages the agent to kick the ball towards a teammate" and "territory, encourages the teammates to spread out such as to control as large a fraction of the pitch as possible" — performed **worse overall** than the standard scheme; the authors concluded shaping rewards that reliably encourage coordinated play can be non-trivial to design and that PBT struggled to optimize the enlarged hyperparameter set (attributed partly to the small population of 16). **This is the central negative result: do not naively pile on cooperative shaping terms.**

**The reward-hacking canon you must avoid.** The classic soccer reward-hacking example (documented in specification-gaming compilations, e.g. Krakovna et al. 2020 / Weng's reward-hacking survey): rewarding ball-touch made an agent vibrate next to the ball to accrue touches. rSoccer's own work (Bassani et al., VSSS-RL) found that adding a ball-position gradient made the SAC agent push the ball toward goal "but with unstable results," and energy penalties had to be balanced against the motion reward. The VMAS football study ("When Is Diversity Rewarded in Cooperative Multi-Agent Learning?", 2025) shows the aggregator *structure* matters: a (min, max) reward structure ("one agent should attend the ball, the other the opponent") yielded positive heterogeneity gains (ΔR = 1.76 ± 0.72), whereas a (mean, mean) structure gave ΔR ≈ 0.01 ± 0.07 — reward structures that force complementary roles beat those rewarding everyone for doing everything.

### Area 2 — Curriculum / training strategies

**Staged skill curriculum (proven in your domain family).** TiZero (Lin et al. 2023) mastered full 11v11 GRF *from scratch, without demonstrations*, via adaptive curriculum learning + a novel self-play strategy + a joint multi-agent objective, improving win rates by over 30% over prior systems and reaching **85.0% win rate, 1.30 assists/game, 0.73 pass rate, 19.2 passes/game, 3.42 goals/game, TrueSkill 45.2**, versus TiKick's 54.3% win rate / 0.65 pass rate / TrueSkill 37.2 (TiZero paper, Table 2). It was **trained over 45 days on a cluster with 800 CPUs and two NVIDIA A100 GPUs** (discount γ = 0.999, Adam lr = 1e-4) — a useful anchor for expected compute. DeepMind's humanoid football used explicit stages: imitation-learn low-level motor skills → mid-level drills (follow, dribble, shoot, kick-to-target) distilled into behavioral priors → multi-agent RL with self-play + PBT.

*Application:* start with 1v0 (rSoccer GoToBall / score on empty goal — GRF calls such scenarios "unit tests"), then 2v2 vs a static/scripted opponent, then 3v3 self-play. Your transformer's token-set design lets you add player tokens as you scale team size without retraining from scratch — exploit this to structure the curriculum. MAT was shown to be an excellent few-shot learner when the number of agents changes, validating this generalization claim.

**Self-play league to prevent collapse/cycling (you already do 80/20; upgrade it).** Your 80% live / 20% frozen split is a reasonable fictitious-self-play approximation. The stronger documented recipe is **AlphaStar-style league training with Prioritized Fictitious Self-Play (PFSP)** (Vinyals et al. 2019): maintain **Main Agents** (≈35% self-play + 50% PFSP against the whole league + ~15% vs previously-beaten-but-now-hard opponents), **Main Exploiters** (trained only against the current main agent to expose its weaknesses), and **League Exploiters** (PFSP vs the whole league; added when they beat all league members >70%). PFSP samples opponents with probability proportional to a function of their win-rate against you, focusing compute on beatable-but-hard opponents instead of wasting games on already-crushed checkpoints; exploiters are periodically reset (AlphaStar reset exploiters to the supervised init with ~25% probability). This is the documented cure for the strategy cycling your 20%-frozen mechanism is meant to prevent.

**Reward annealing dense→sparse (the PBT reference implementation).** DeepMind's approach does not hand-script the anneal; the population-based outer loop *evolves* the shaping coefficients and discounts online, driven by match results, so agents automatically shift weight from myopic dense shaping toward the long-horizon team objective. Each shaping channel ℓ has its **own coefficient α_{k,ℓ} AND its own discount factor γ_{k,ℓ}** (surrogate objective J = E[Σ_ℓ α_{k,ℓ} Σ_t γ_{k,ℓ}^t r̂^{(k,ℓ)}]), and the PBT outer loop evolves α, the per-channel γ, the drill-prior mixture weights, and learning rates. If PBT is too expensive, a simpler linear anneal of the *non-potential* shaping weights toward zero over training approximates this; leave the PBRS potential terms on permanently (they are policy-invariant and need no annealing).

**Curriculum on opponent difficulty / pitch.** GRF's Football Academy is a difficulty-graded scenario suite; TiZero advanced curriculum stages using win-rate thresholds (~75–80%). Use a win-rate gate — e.g., advance when the live policy beats the current stage opponent >70–75% over N games.

### Area 3 — Architecture / attention mechanisms

**MAT (Multi-Agent Transformer, Wen et al. 2022, arXiv:2205.14953) is the closest published analog to your design.** It casts cooperative MARL as sequence modeling with an encoder–decoder transformer, leveraging the **multi-agent advantage decomposition theorem** to turn joint-policy optimization into a sequential decision process with a monotonic-improvement guarantee and linear complexity in the number of agents. It is trained online (unlike Decision Transformer), outperforms MAPPO and HAPPO on SMAC, Multi-Agent MuJoCo, and GRF, and is an excellent few-shot learner when the number of agents changes. Your setup emits all actions in one forward pass rather than autoregressively; consider MAT's **autoregressive decoder** as an option, because conditioning each robot's action on teammates' already-chosen actions is exactly what breaks the symmetric "everyone chases the ball" equilibrium.

**UPDeT (Hu et al. 2021, ICLR spotlight, arXiv:2101.08001)** is the other key reference: a transformer that decouples each action-group's policy from matched observation entities via self-attention, explicitly built so one architecture handles 3v3, 5v6, etc., with strong transfer (reported ~10× faster training on transfer). It corroborates the token-per-entity design and ships a PyMARL-based implementation you can mine.

**Auxiliary losses for role differentiation / information sharing:**
- **ROMA (Wang et al. 2020, arXiv:2003.08039):** learn a stochastic role embedding conditioned on observation-history, with two regularizers — an *identifiability* term (mutual information between role and behavior) and a *specialization* term (agents with similar roles specialize on similar subtasks). Add this as an auxiliary head on your per-token representations to push the three robots toward differentiated roles (attacker/support/defender) rather than identical ball-seekers.
- **RODE (Wang et al. 2021):** associate each role with a restricted action subspace, shrinking the joint action space and easing role emergence.
- **ACORM / CIA (contrastive role representations, 2023–2024):** use contrastive learning to differentiate agents by credit/contribution or behavioral pattern — directly attacks homogeneity.

Because your policy shares weights across tokens, roles will **not** emerge from the architecture alone (weight-sharing biases toward identical policies). An explicit role/diversity auxiliary loss, or a per-token role/index embedding, is the mechanism to break that symmetry.

### Area 4 — Credit assignment

The core problem: with a shared team reward, the robot that scores and the robot that made the key pass or decoy run get the same signal, so individual contribution is invisible. Options, roughly by implementation cost:

- **COMA (Foerster et al. 2018, AAAI):** centralized critic + a **counterfactual baseline** that marginalizes out one agent's action while fixing the others; the per-agent advantage isolates that agent's contribution, and the baseline's expected contribution to the gradient is zero (variance reduction without bias). A natural fit for a centralized joint-action policy — your critic already sees joint state and joint action, and COMA's critic computes Q for all of one agent's actions in a single forward pass.
- **Difference rewards (Tumer & Agogino):** D_a = r(s,u) − r(s,(u_{−a}, c_a)) compares global reward to reward with agent a replaced by a default action; any action improving D_a improves the true global reward. Requires a simulator or reward model for the counterfactual — you *have* a simulator, so this is feasible.
- **Potential-based difference rewards (Devlin, Yliniemi, Kudenko & Tumer 2014, AAMAS):** "Counterfactual as Potential" (CaP) and "Difference Rewards incorporating Potential-Based shaping" (DRiP) combine both — CaP applies PBRS-style guarantees to difference rewards without domain knowledge; reported convergence up to 23.8× faster, or joint policies up to 196% better, than difference rewards alone. This is the cleanest marriage of your PBRS backbone with credit assignment.
- **Value decomposition — VDN / QMIX (Sunehag 2017; Rashid et al. 2018/2020):** decompose team value into per-agent utilities; QMIX enforces ∂Q_tot/∂Q_a ≥ 0 via a monotonic mixing network with hypernetwork-generated non-negative weights, guaranteeing consistency between centralized training and decentralized argmax. These are value-based; your PPO/actor-critic analog is a centralized critic with per-agent advantages (MAPPO/MAT territory). Note a documented VDN soccer failure: in a GRF case study, VDN defenders all pushed toward the ball-carrier and ignored an off-ball runner who received an easy pass — a credit-assignment failure directly analogous to your ball-chasing collapse.
- **Shapley-value credit (SQDDPG, Wang et al. 2020, arXiv:1907.05707; SHAQ; Shapley Counterfactual Credits 2021):** distribute team reward by each agent's marginal contribution across coalitions, approximated by Monte-Carlo sampling (Shapley Counterfactual Credits found ~4–5 samples sufficient). Fairest but most expensive in general — however, with only **3 agents the exact Shapley computation is cheap (3! = 6 orderings)**, an unusual opportunity your small team size affords.

**Soccer-analytics-inspired credit signals.** Sports analytics offers ready-made "value of an action/position" functions usable as auxiliary reward or credit:
- **Expected Possession Value (EPV)** (Fernández, Bornn & Cervone 2019), **VAEP** (Decroos et al. 2019), and **Expected Threat (xT)** (Singh) estimate the probability a game-state or action leads to a goal; the open-source `socceraction` library (KU Leuven) implements xT and VAEP.
- The GRF case study "Embedding Contextual Information through Reward Shaping" (arXiv:2303.15471) combined a **Pitch Control Function** with an **EPV** grid into a single "game-state EPV" scalar — EPV_state = Σ_ij PitchControl_ij × EPV_ij (their Eq. 4) — added to the sparse goal-difference reward. On a defensive scenario (4 controlled defenders vs 6 built-in attackers) at the highest difficulty (0.95), the shaped VDN reached an average goal difference of −0.15 versus plain VDN's −0.40 (5 seeds, 2M steps); improvements held at medium (−0.12 vs −0.23) and easy (−0.07 vs −0.12). This demonstrates an EPV-derived dense signal that rewards off-ball positioning. Because EPV/xT are functions of state, using them as a PBRS potential Φ = EPV(s) preserves the policy-invariance guarantee.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Instrument first.** Log division-of-labour and passing metrics (pass count, pass rate, assists, teammate spread, and per-entity counterfactual policy divergence) from day one, exactly as DeepMind did — you cannot detect the ball-chasing collapse or its resolution otherwise. Continuously benchmark against the single-agent rSoccer policy; you must beat it (the published multi-agent baseline lost to it).

**Stage 1 — PBRS backbone + copied constants.**
- PBRS potentials (policy-invariant, always on): Φ_ball = −‖ball−oppgoal‖; Φ_spacing (teammate spread / target formation); optionally Φ = EPV(state).
- Small non-potential incentives copied from TiZero: pass-before-goal +0.05, holding-ball +0.0001/step, grouping penalty −0.001; sparse ±1 scoring. Alternatively use GRF CHECKPOINT (+0.1 × 10 regions, capped at +1).
- Keep the summed non-potential shaping bounded well below the scoring reward's scale, and plan to anneal it toward zero.

**Stage 2 — Curriculum + league.** 1v0 → 2v2 vs scripted → 3v3 self-play, gated by a >70–75% win-rate threshold. Upgrade the 80/20 self-play to PFSP with at least one Main Exploiter to prevent cycling; sample frozen opponents proportional to their win-rate against the live policy.

**Stage 3 — Credit assignment + role auxiliary.** Add a COMA-style counterfactual advantage in the centralized critic (cheap for 3 agents); because n=3, also consider *exact* Shapley credit (6 orderings). Add a ROMA-style role auxiliary loss or per-token role embeddings to break weight-sharing symmetry and induce attacker/support/defender specialization. Consider MAT-style autoregressive action decoding so each robot's action conditions on teammates' chosen actions.

**Stage 4 — Anneal to the true objective / PBT.** If compute allows, wrap shaping-weight and discount hyperparameters in a PBT outer loop (as DeepMind did) so the system evolves from dense shaping toward sparse scoring automatically. Otherwise linearly anneal the non-potential shaping toward zero over training.

**Thresholds that change the plan:**
- Teammate-spread stays low and pass rate ≈ 0 after Stage 1 → shaping is being ignored or hacked; increase the *potential-based* spacing weight (safe) before touching non-potential bonuses, and adopt a (min, max)-style complementary-role reward structure.
- Win-rate oscillates against frozen checkpoints (strategy cycling) → add/strengthen League Exploiters and increase PFSP prioritization.
- Roles stay homogeneous despite the role auxiliary loss → move to RODE-style per-role action-subspace restriction, or introduce a small per-role parameter set alongside the shared trunk.
- Adding cooperative shaping *lowers* scoring (the DeepMind failure) → remove the added team shaping term; it is very likely mis-scaled or inducing a degenerate equilibrium.

## Caveats
- **Weight-sharing fights specialization.** A single shared-weight transformer biases all three robots toward identical policies; genuine role specialization requires an explicit mechanism (role embeddings, diversity/role auxiliary loss, or per-role parameters). Do not expect roles for free.
- **Most cited results are from adjacent domains.** GRF, SMAC, and DeepMind humanoid football are not VSS; the constants (0.05 pass bonus, 0.1 checkpoints, 10 regions, −0.001 grouping) are strong starting points but will need retuning for VSS's smaller field, differential-drive dynamics, and 3v3 scale.
- **Self-play + shaping interact, and emergence is slow.** DeepMind's evidence is that coordination emerged from *competition automatically re-weighting shaping*, not shaping alone — and even there it took ~1.5 billion steps. Your zero-sum self-play is an asset, but budget for long training and resist over-tuning shaping to force early coordination.
- **PBRS caveats.** PBRS preserves optimal policies only if F is exactly γΦ(s′) − Φ(s) with the *same* γ as the MDP; using per-channel discounts (DeepMind-style) breaks the strict guarantee, so treat those channels as ordinary (annealable) shaping, not policy-invariant.
- **EPV/xT as reward introduces a learned model into the loop**, and its errors become reward-hacking surfaces; using it as a PBRS potential mitigates but does not eliminate this.
- **Some specific magnitudes are unreported in the literature:** the exact weighting coefficient balancing "game-state EPV" against goal-difference in arXiv:2303.15471, and the exact initial α/γ/mixture ranges in DeepMind's Appendix B.5/B.7, are not given in those papers' main text; the DeepMind ablation's precise quantitative degradation (a "~30% lower ball-to-goal velocity" figure) could not be verified and should be treated as unconfirmed — the qualitative result (added team shaping performed worse) is confirmed. Treat all such magnitudes as starting points requiring your own tuning.
